# Cross-domain applications

These are illustrative planning examples, not source-verified finished lessons. Research factual claims and safety conditions for an actual production. None requires the original conference topic, voice, palette, or slide count.

## Practical lesson: urban gardening

**Brief:** Beginners, 12-minute recording, explain how to investigate a struggling balcony plant.

**Outcome:** Learners can gather observations and choose a next diagnostic step instead of guessing from one symptom.

**Structure:** A plant looks unhealthy → the same visible symptom can have different causes → inspect conditions with a clear sequence → compare two constructed cases → choose a next observation for a new case.

**Visual plan:** Real plant and container views, labeled observations, side-by-side cases that change one relevant factor, a simple decision diagram. Avoid a parade of product recommendations.

**Evidence and scope:** Verify plant-specific care claims. Label constructed cases. Do not claim one rule applies to every species or climate.

**Transfer prompt:** Given a different plant and a short observation log, identify the next useful observation and explain what it would distinguish.

## Humanities talk: interpreting an industrial-era city

**Brief:** An 18-minute history lesson using a map, a worker's account, and a municipal report supplied by the instructor.

**Outcome:** Learners distinguish what each source can establish and compare two interpretations of the same change.

**Structure:** One central historical question → source context and perspective → inspect specific evidence → compare interpretations → identify what additional evidence would change the conclusion.

**Visual plan:** Readable crops of permission-cleared sources; map locations highlighted when named; chronology separated from causal interpretation. Keep attribution adjacent to the source.

**Evidence and scope:** Verify dates, provenance, translation, and the instructor's requested period. Do not fabricate quotations or turn an illustrative map into an archival object.

**Transfer prompt:** Offer a fourth source and ask which claim it supports, complicates, or cannot address.

## Software walkthrough: finding duplicate records

**Brief:** A 10-minute beginner demonstration in the user's chosen spreadsheet tool.

**Outcome:** Learners can identify duplicate records using a stated key, preview the result, and preserve the original data.

**Structure:** Same-name records may or may not be duplicates → define the record key → demonstrate a preview → inspect edge cases → apply to a new small dataset.

**Visual plan:** Use an actual application recording with fictional data, readable zoom, cursor focus, and explicit before/after states. Show the precise selection and undo/recovery route.

**Evidence and scope:** Verify current interface behavior locally or from official documentation. Do not represent a designed mockup as an executed operation.

**Transfer prompt:** Change the key from name alone to name plus date and explain why the result changes.

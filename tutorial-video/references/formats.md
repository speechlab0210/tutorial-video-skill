# Adapt the workflow to the talk

| Format | Organizing question | Best evidence of understanding | Production emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introductory lesson | What is this, and when would I use it? | Worked example plus a changed case | Prerequisites, concrete actors, paced reveals |
| Advanced research tutorial | Which problem does each method solve, under which conditions? | Compare methods on one task and explain limits | Primary sources, definitions, fair quantitative comparisons |
| Software walkthrough | Can the learner complete this task and recover from a likely failure? | Execute the same workflow from a clean start | Real UI, readable zoom, state/version, reproducible inputs |
| Practical or physical skill | What action, observation, and correction are required? | Perform the steps and diagnose a common mistake | Camera angle, hands/tools visible, real-time demonstration |
| History or humanities | How do evidence and perspective change an interpretation? | Compare readings of the same source | Context, chronology, attribution, competing interpretations |
| Business or policy briefing | What decision follows from these assumptions and tradeoffs? | Choose an option and justify it | Scenarios, uncertainty, actual alternatives, scope |
| Workshop | What can participants build or practice during the session? | Learner artifact and feedback | Setup checks, exercise time, pause points, recovery paths |
| Keynote or short public talk | Which central idea should the audience remember and reconsider? | Explain the idea with a fresh example | Few claims, memorable example, a coherent ending |

Do not force every format into a research survey, an exam, or a slide monologue. A lecture can include a screen demonstration or physical example when it serves the learning goal.

## Existing material

An instructor's deck may already have a strong structure. Preserve it and repair the requested weaknesses. A historical source order, proof sequence, or procedure may be essential; reorganizing it around generic “capabilities” can make it worse.

For a live lecture adaptation, distinguish the words captured in the recording from edits to a new narration script. A transcript correction must not falsely imply the speaker said a revised claim. Label corrected editions or rerecord the authorized passage.

## Multi-part and multilingual courses

Provide a shared glossary, design system, prerequisite map, and version manifest. End each part with its answer and a reason to continue. End the whole course by revisiting all major outcomes.

Localize examples and vocabulary for the intended audience. Keep factual claims equivalent, but allow different pacing and sentence structures. Do not reuse one language's subtitle times on another language's audio without alignment.
